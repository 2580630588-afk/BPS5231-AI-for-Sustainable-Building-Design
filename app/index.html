Office Plan and Window Option Comparison

Enter two design cases to compare annual cooling thermal demand and the relative effect of window interventions. Predictions assume Singapore typical weather, fixed operation and an open-plan intermediate floor. Values do not represent building electricity use or cost.

Baseline: SN75 glazing without an overhang. Six options use linked manufacturer optical and thermal parameters. Predictions and completed simulations are labelled separately.

How to use the results

Compare plan forms first, then compare glazing and shading within the same form. An out-of-domain warning indicates that a new EnergyPlus simulation is appropriate; it is not a statistical confidence interval. The circle is an equal-area 24-sided approximation, and the concave L plan does not represent every complex floor plate.

Operating assumptions and daylight limits

Floor-to-floor height is 3 m, with adiabatic floor and roof. Outdoor air is 0.6 ACH, the weekday schedule is 09:00–18:00, the cooling setpoint is 26°C, relative-humidity control is 40–70%, and HVAC uses ideal loads. Daylight dimming is disabled.

Rectangular daylight evidence covers the original 2:1, 900 m² cases at 0° or 45° and WWR 20%/40%/60%, plus 1.5:1 at 850 m² and 30° and 3:1 at 1100 m² and 60°, both at WWR 20%/40%. Nonrectangular daylight evidence covers the wide-arm L, triangle, pentagon, hexagon and 24-gon circle approximation at 850 m², 30° and WWR 20%/40%. Rectangles use a nine-point mean. Nonrectangular cases use an area-weighted clipped grid, with a 2 m grid auditing the 4 m grid. Neither metric is the percentage of floor area that complies with a daylight standard. The 50% screen is a study criterion, not a code check.
